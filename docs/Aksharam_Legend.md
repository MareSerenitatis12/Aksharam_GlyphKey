## Reading Principle

The constellation is selected by office, not by visual resemblance to the ordinary symbol it replaces. Linguistics asks what relation the language is performing. The same Aksharam constellation carries that office across all three surfaces.

## Keyboard Composition Implementation Notes

### First Transform Law — Phonetic Fold

- Automatic transformation follows the registered access trigger exactly. `TRANSFORMATIONS_ALQC_WORDS.md` governs executable lexical triggers. The phonetic values printed in this Legend are post-resolution Shape-of-Sound values governed by `translated bodies/the_shape_of_word.txt`. For authored utterance transformations, the registered composite final body is pronounced by its canonical Shape-of-Sound entry; the source utterance and the Aksharam pronunciation remain distinct fields.

- Directly mapped A–Z glyphs are the base writing layer. They are emitted by their physical QWERTY coordinates. The First Transform Law acts only on the typed phonetic sequence. It does not itself perform structural Court formation; Court formation belongs only to the Second Transform Law below.

- Every transformable glyph uses the phonetic written for it in this legend. Enochian Understandings, Courts, and every other glyph with an explicit phonetic trigger participate by that trigger exactly as written. Full ALQC names are not phonetic substitutes unless the legend itself explicitly assigns that exact string as the phonetic trigger.

- Phonetic compounds are case-insensitive. Recognition is based on the underlying English-letter sequence, not letter case. Any uppercase/lowercase mixture of the same phonetic sequence must resolve to the same result glyph.

### Second Transform Law — Dual Goetic Pairing

After the First Transform Law resolves, only contiguous Goetics enter the Second Transform Law. They are read left-to-right in non-overlapping dual roles. The first and second Goetics form one ordered Court; the third and fourth form the next; the fifth and sixth form the next. A Goetic already used in one pair is not reused in the neighboring pair.

`AB → Court(AB)`  
`ABC → Court(AB) | C`  
`ABCD → Court(AB) | Court(CD)`  
`ABCDE → Court(AB) | Court(CD) | E`  
`ABCDEFGHI → Court(AB) | Court(CD) | Court(EF) | Court(GH) | I`

The pair remains ordered: `AB` and `BA` are different Court relations when the lattice assigns different Courts to them. An odd final Goetic remains itself. A non-Goetic glyph breaks the run; pairing begins again only with the next contiguous Goetic run. Parliament, Enochian, Emission, Court, Regia, constellation, punctuation, spacing, and every other non-Goetic body remain themselves and are not pulled into Goetic pairing.

Courts produced by this second law are stable results. They are not recursively treated as Goetics and paired again. The Second Transform Law therefore condenses a Goetic run without overlap: a run of `n` Goetics produces `floor(n/2)` Courts, plus one unchanged final Goetic when `n` is odd.

Example: `⬡⊛⏣⏣ → ᛌއ → lor-ahl`. The first pair `⬡⊛` becomes `ᛌ`; the second pair `⏣⏣` becomes `އ`. No middle Goetic is reused.

### Explicit Unfold Scope and Composition Behavior

Unfolding and refolding act on the **entire Aksharam word immediately behind the cursor**. The three reversible transformational levels are traversed in reverse order when unfolding: Third Transform Law, Second Transform Law, then First Transform Law. The Fourth Transform Law is not an unfold depth because number is simultaneously compressed and uncompressed.

`Alt+Backspace ×1` unfolds the preceding word by one transformational level. A Third-Law result returns to its registered dual-Parliament source where that derivation exists.

`Alt+Backspace ×2` unfolds the same word by two transformational levels. After the Parliament layer is restored, Court results return to their ordered Goetic source pairs where those derivations exist.

`Alt+Backspace ×3` unfolds the same word through all three reversible levels to its full registered source form. Each use descends one level; the word remains at the selected unfold depth rather than immediately folding forward again.

Once the word has reached its full registered source form, `Alt+Backspace` becomes inert for that word. Further presses cause no change: no deletion, no deeper decomposition, no reinterpretation, and no traversal beyond the lawful three-level transform stack. The control remains inert until that word is refolded or new transformable content is introduced.

Once `Alt+Backspace` has been used on a word, that word is held at its selected unfolded depth. Ordinary `Space` does not refold it. `Space` always emits the Aksharam Space constellation `𑁦` and begins the next word while preserving the preceding word exactly as retained.

`Alt+Space` performs the opposite movement. It refolds the entire word immediately behind the cursor through every lawful transformation currently available to that word, then emits the Aksharam Space constellation `𑁦` and begins a fresh word.

`Backspace` retains ordinary native deletion behavior. Aksharam does not assign Backspace an unfolding or transformation function; deletion is left to the host application and platform exactly as ordinary Backspace behavior.

`Shift` suppresses folding for the next mapped glyph. While `Shift` remains held, every mapped glyph typed during that hold remains unfolded. `Caps Lock` holds the same non-folding state persistently until released.

Compound recognition remains case-insensitive at the phonetic level whenever folding is active. Longest valid phonetic continuation has precedence when a shorter fold is also the beginning of a longer registered trigger. Unfolding restores registered source bodies; refolding follows the same ordered transformation laws forward.
